# Alter-course pull-out at plough launch

**Design proposal, 9 November 2026.** Add an *A/C pull-out* mode to Span & Slack Analysis. Start with a specific decision: **at this launch KP and estimated bottom tension, which previously laid bends might move, and towards which constraint?** The first release should screen for the start of movement. A subsequent solver can estimate displaced cable paths for stated assumptions. Both should run from theory and user-entered scenarios, without operational records.

## What the plugin can do today

The A/C finding already calculates a holding radius and multiplies it by the turn angle in radians. | That product is **arc length at the limiting radius**, not lateral offset, length of cable drawn into the plough, or distance a launch load travels. The cable cannot leave the input line in plan. |

**A useful run now:** run the surface-lay case first. Duplicate it, set the lay direction, and use a pinned range starting at the trial PLDN in that direction, with the estimated pull on the laid side as its entry tension. Compare the residual tension, A/C note and spans on the *previously laid* side. This represents an idealised entry load followed by a hold. It does not simulate the launch sequence or draw the displaced cable.

## What can be calculated from an estimated tension

There are two separate questions. **Does the extra pull reach the bend?** Axial resistance governs how much tension is transmitted. **Can the bend hold the tension that arrives?** The cable pulls towards the inside of the turn; lateral resistance must supply the opposing force. Use the tension **at the bend**, not the value at the plough.

For a taut, flexible cable on a level bed, in continuous contact, with submerged weight `w` in N/m and tensions in N:

- On a straight entry leg under an increasing pull, assume an initially uniform residual tension `T0`, a launch tension `TL ≥ T0`, and fully mobilised axial resistance `qa = μa w` in the sliding length. At distance `d` back from the launch, `T(d) = max(T0, TL − qa d)`; the theoretical reach of the **increase** is `(TL − T0) / qa`. This is a straight-leg idealisation, not a universal setback. With zero axial resistance there is no friction-limited reach.
- At a smooth bend of actual or assumed radius `R`, the local transverse demand is `T / R` N/m. For simple Coulomb contact the available lateral resistance alone is `ql = μl w`. The unfactored holding radius is `Rhold = T / ql`, or equivalently `Thold = ql R`. This is distinct from the manufacturer's minimum bend radius (MBR): one concerns staying in position; the other concerns the cable's mechanical limit. [Worzyk, §7.1.4.1, eq. 7.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-01270-9_7) gives a cable-laying design form with an explicit safety factor; keep any project factor separate from the physical threshold.
- A demand greater than the available resistance rules out that fixed bend **under those assumptions**. A smaller demand is not proof of stability: axial and lateral friction may both be mobilised; slopes, contact loss, initial slack, initial radius and embedment can change the result. A sharp RPL vertex supplies an angle but no physical radius. Without a stated radius or resolved curve, return the required radius, not a displacement or a pass.

Turn angle still matters: for equal tension on each leg the net pull across the whole bend is `2 T sin(|turn|/2)` (angle in radians). A 5° and a 60° bend at the same radius have the same local demand per metre, but different total force and exposed length. Nearby reverse turns can also draw on the same cable. With zero lateral resistance, a taut, curved string on a flat bed cannot hold its curve without another restraint; report that explicitly.

**Synthetic benchmark: a 200 m radius bend with a 250 m straight lead to the launch.** Assume `w = 50 N/m`, `μa = 0.40`, `μl = 0.50`, and `T0 = 2 kN`. These are example inputs, not soil recommendations. The straight lead sheds up to 5 kN; the bend can resist 25 N/m laterally, equivalent to 5 kN at this radius.

| Estimated launch pull | Axial reach on a straight bed | Tension arriving at bend | Lateral demand at bend | Required holding radius |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 kN | 300 m | 3 kN | 15 N/m | 120 m |
| 10 kN | 400 m | 5 kN | 25 N/m | 200 m |
| 12 kN | 500 m | 7 kN | 35 N/m | 280 m |

This example crosses the lateral-only threshold at a **launch** pull of 10 kN. At 12 kN a longer straight lead in the same model would transmit less load; it does not follow that a particular setback is safe. The current engine reproduced the three bend-station tensions and holding radii on a synthetic 1 m profile (PLDN KP 1.200, A/C station KP 0.950, lay towards increasing KP). Its reported pull-back lengths were 294, 394 and 494 m: that output counts a tension rise above a reporting tolerance, whereas the table includes any rise. Neither length is sideways travel.

## From a screen to a path

**1. Exposed-bend screen — first release.** Add a launch event separate from a burial hold. Enter the KP and lay direction, pre-launch residual/deposit state, and low/base/high estimates of cable tension on the already-laid side at the bed. A touchdown or plough-exit estimate must identify its location and direction; tow-wire load and deck cable tension are different quantities. The [OOI seaplow account](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/07/2011/seaplow-101/) illustrates the separate tow, umbilical and product cable paths. Run the baseline and launch cases separately, keeping the state *at the event*; later lay must not silently change what is reported for launch.

Use Span & Slack's local tension and contact results. Supply smooth initial radii from a chosen route or an explicit scenario; bracket plausible radii where geometry is uncertain. Evaluate resistance over resolved contact lengths, with force per metre distinguished from a concentrated support reaction. On relief, the solver's vertical reaction on a fixed profile is a screening clue, not the true 3D bed-normal pressure. Flag spans, cross-slope, nearby supports, interacting bends or unresolved radius for further assessment. Display each A/C's KP, lead distance, local tension before/at launch, radius required vs assumed, and the scenarios in which lateral demand exceeds resistance. Map the affected reference reaches. Do not draw a displaced line at this stage.

**2. Plan-view displacement — the first release that draws a path.** Start with a short window of exposed cable on flat ground. Use material nodes (fixed identities along the cable), their unstretched lengths, a smooth initial centreline, initial tension/slack, and an explicit far-end condition. First verify that the assumed initial shape can hold its own load; otherwise report baseline movement separately. Apply increasing launch load in small steps and retain the previous stuck/sliding state. Then unload if specified: peak excursion and final residual offset need not coincide. [Orcina's statics documentation](https://www.orcina.com/webhelp/OrcaFlex/Content/html/Linedata%2CStatics.htm) explains why a static end tension alone does not select a unique frictional equilibrium.

For an initial force-controlled benchmark, restrain the far end and pull the launch-end material node along its allowed guide direction; its travel is an **output**. For a stationary plough exit, use a guide through which cable can pass, with a defined source/sink of cable. If the known control is metres taken up while lowering the plough, prescribe take-up and solve for force instead. Do not prescribe force and displacement to the same degree of freedom. An estimated tension can drive the model without a payout log. It cannot also establish how much spare cable was already on the bed: run explicit slack/rest-length cases, or withhold metre offsets. Use inextensible elements with length constraints, or a documented axial stiffness `EA`; bending stiffness `EI` and tensile ratings are not `EA`.

Use a tension-only string with directional seabed resistance and incremental stick/slip. For the simple friction option, use one stated combined limit (e.g. an ellipse in axial and lateral force) so a contact cannot expend its full friction twice. Offer force–displacement laws with peak breakout and residual resistance when those inputs are available; use low/base/high assumptions separately for axial and lateral resistance. [Griffiths et al.'s lateral experiments](https://doi.org/10.1139/cgj-2018-0208) and [axial experiments](https://doi.org/10.1115/OMAE2024-127137) show why rock roughness relative to cable/pipe diameter matters; these are not transferable sand/clay design coefficients. [Orcina's resistance model](https://www.orcina.com/webhelp/OrcaFlex/Content/html/Linetheory%2CSeabedtangentialresistance.htm) is a reference for breakout, residual resistance and displacement history, not a validation of this solver.

Show the initial line, the actual step with the worst clearance, and the residual line after unloading. Report signed movement, material length drawn towards the launch, and minimum clearance to supplied hazards over all increments. Map the area swept by the **tested scenarios**; it is not a confidence interval. Do not join pointwise maxima from different steps into a fictitious cable. Record both movement from the initial cable and departure from the design RPL. If a sliding zone reaches an artificial window end, expand the domain or report it unresolved. If there is no bounded equilibrium, slack localisation is indeterminate, or results change materially with discretisation, withhold the predicted line.

**3. Combined span and pull-out — local 3D contact.** A depression inside a bend could let a straightening cable bridge a hollow. The suspended portion then supplies no seabed friction, while its shoulders can carry more reaction. Movement onto a shallower shoulder can have the opposite effect. This is why simply draping a displaced XY line over a raster, or adding two independent offsets, will give misleading results.

Solve positions in XYZ over a bathymetric swath, with tension, submerged weight, bed-normal reaction and tangential resistance in equilibrium. Enforce non-penetration, compressive-only contact, and zero bed friction in a free span. Resolve gravity down the slope and the cable's curvature in the **local seabed tangent plane**. A first coupling experiment can alternate plan equilibrium and the existing vertical span solve on the displaced line, resampling bathymetry and updating contact forces each iteration. Accept it only if force balance, contact state, cable length and offsets converge against independent benchmarks; use a coupled spatial string/rod solver otherwise. Include bending stiffness locally when tight curvature, bodies or support checks need it. A profile without cross-track coverage can run the original span analysis or an explicitly flat case; it cannot support a terrain-based displaced path.

Treat confirmed anchors, exposed cable, new furrow and established burial as different states. A fresh trench is not automatically a clamp; bracket a free/held transition if its capacity is unknown. Extracting already-buried cable needs independent lateral/uplift soil laws. Vessel heave, snatch loading and plough towing dynamics are outside this quasi-static model. General surface lay can later feed in a prescribed touchdown track, bottom-tension history and cable deposition; predicting touchdown from **vessel** track, currents and payout is a separate water-column model. [Zajac's laying and recovery analysis](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1957.tb01505.x) and [Makai's installation-control paper](https://www.zttcable.com.hk/en/download/suboptic07/CABLE%20ROUTE%20PLANNING%20AND%20INSTALLATION%20CONTROL.pdf) are useful for that boundary.

## Plugin implementation and acceptance

- **One workflow, separate mechanics.** Add *A/C pull-out* to Span & Slack with the same route, cable, scenarios, plots and sweeps. Enter from Burial Planner with “Assess pull-out at this PLDN”, passing the selected plan, route/KP basis, direction and event. Keep the existing 2D solve available and unchanged. Put the pure numerical code beside `cable_seabed/planning.py`; use the existing [cancellable task](../span_analysis/task.py) and [scenario store](../span_analysis/store.py) via adapters.
- **Keep coordinate systems and histories explicit.** Solve in a local metric frame, preserve reference RPL KP, and store material chainage separately from projected position on the route. Use the existing cross-course sign (+ starboard looking towards increasing KP), also when laying in reverse. Snapshot initial geometry, estimated loads/sequence, resistance assumptions, boundary conditions, bathymetry source/resolution/datum, solver version and convergence. Save predicted cable lines separately from surveyed, RPL, tool and span-finding layers. Invalidate cached results when inputs change.
- **Verify the screen before adding a path.** Recover the straight axial tension gradient, the constant-tension circular bend threshold, and the numerical example above. Reverse lay direction; only the previously exposed side should see the launch. A literal RPL corner must never yield a fabricated radius. Distinguish “below the assumed lateral-only threshold”, “exceeds it” and “not assessed”.
- **Verify displacement before releasing metre offsets.** Use static equilibrium below breakout; mirror-image left/right bends; a frictionless taut string between compatible supports; an explicitly fixed anchor; neighbouring opposite bends; loading and unloading with slack take-up. Check equilibrium residuals, no compressive string force, the joint friction limit, material-length balance including end exchange/stretch, and convergence with finer mesh, smaller load increments and a longer domain. Compare a synthetic bend case with an independent solver using identical start state and boundary conditions.
- **Verify 3D before calling the results coupled.** Recover the current vertical span benchmarks on a straight alignment and the planar solution on a flat bed. Test a hollow inside a bend, a span with zero bed traction, a cross-slope, opposite lay directions and a swath with missing cells. Publish numerical tolerances and comparison results for the features being released. Analytical or cross-solver agreement is verification; no field accuracy or operational tension limit is established by these tests.

**First implementation ticket:** make a synthetic launch scenario and an A/C results table in Span & Slack, with load and launch-KP sweeps, a radius input and links to the existing span findings. It is useful on its own and provides a repeatable benchmark for the solver that eventually draws cable paths.
